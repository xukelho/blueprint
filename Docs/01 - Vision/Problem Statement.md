# Background

Architectural projects involve continuous collaboration between architects and clients.

A single project may generate dozens of design revisions, hundreds of comments, and many decisions over several months.

Despite this, communication is often spread across multiple disconnected platforms:

- Email
- WhatsApp
- Phone calls
- PDF annotations
- Printed drawings
- Face-to-face meetings
- Cloud storage folders

Each communication channel contains only part of the project's history.

As the project grows, understanding what was decided—and why—becomes increasingly difficult.

---

# Core Problems

## Fragmented Communication

Project discussions are distributed across multiple communication channels.

A decision discussed during a meeting may later be confirmed by email, modified via WhatsApp, and finally reflected in a revised drawing.

There is no single location where the complete conversation can be understood.

As a result:

- important information becomes difficult to locate
- decisions are easily forgotten
- misunderstandings become more frequent

---

## Poor Revision Traceability

Architectural projects naturally evolve through multiple revisions.

Version 3 may contain twenty small modifications compared to Version 2, but those changes are rarely documented in a structured way.

Clients often ask:

> "What exactly changed?"

Architects frequently need to manually explain every revision.

This process is repetitive, time-consuming, and prone to errors.

---

## Feedback Without Context

Clients usually provide feedback using screenshots, printed drawings, emails, or messaging applications.

Examples include:

> "Move this wall slightly."

> "I don't like this room."

> "Can we make this bigger?"

Months later, it may no longer be clear:

- which drawing was being discussed
- which revision the comment referred to
- whether the issue was resolved
- why a decision was made

The context surrounding the feedback is gradually lost.

---

## Lack of Decision History

Architectural decisions are rarely documented alongside the project itself.

Over time it becomes difficult to answer questions such as:

- Who requested this change?
- When was it approved?
- Why was it changed?
- Was this decision ever challenged?
- Is this still the latest approved solution?

Without a structured history, project knowledge depends heavily on individual memory.

---

## Difficult Client Experience

Most clients are not architects.

They struggle to understand:

- revision numbers
- drawing conventions
- technical documentation
- project progress

This creates unnecessary friction during the collaboration process.

Clients often feel disconnected from the evolution of their own project.

---

## Repetitive Administrative Work

Architects spend significant time on activities that do not directly contribute to design.

Examples include:

- explaining revisions
- searching old emails
- locating client feedback
- confirming approvals
- tracking pending requests
- organising project folders

These activities consume valuable time without adding architectural value.

---

# Consequences

These problems lead to:

- slower project iterations
- duplicated discussions
- missed requirements
- client frustration
- reduced transparency
- increased project risk

Although each issue may appear small in isolation, together they significantly reduce the efficiency of the design process.

---

# Why Existing Tools Are Not Enough

Several categories of software already exist within the architecture industry.

However, they primarily focus on creating designs rather than managing collaboration.

CAD and BIM software are excellent design tools.

Cloud storage platforms organise files.

Messaging applications facilitate conversations.

Project management software organises tasks.

None of these solutions provide a complete collaboration history centred around architectural revisions.

The missing component is not another drawing tool.

The missing component is structured communication.

---

# Opportunity

Software development underwent a similar transformation.

Version control systems replaced manually sharing source code.

Platforms such as GitHub combined version history, discussions, approvals, issue tracking, and collaboration into a single workflow.

Architecture still lacks an equivalent platform focused on client collaboration.

Blueprint aims to fill that gap.

---

# Problem Statement

Architectural communication is fragmented, difficult to trace, and heavily dependent on disconnected tools.

This creates unnecessary administrative work, increases the risk of misunderstandings, and reduces transparency for both architects and clients.

The industry needs a collaboration platform that centralises revisions, discussions, approvals, and project history without replacing the professional design software architects already use.

Blueprint exists to solve that problem.
